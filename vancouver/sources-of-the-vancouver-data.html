Sources of the Vancouver data

Here are the sources of the Vancouver data. This site is not the City of Vancouver and has no official status. If anything differs, the City’s own record prevails — it is one click away on every card. These sources feed the decisions of Vancouver City Council and the members’ pages.

Where the data comes from

  • Decisions, votes, meetings and members all come from one data set published by the City on its open data portal: “Council voting records”. Since April 2016, Council votes with an electronic system, and each line of the data set is one member’s vote on one item: the meeting, its type, the date, the agenda line of the item, the member, the vote (In Favour, In Opposition, Abstain, Absent, Declared Conflict, Ineligible, No Vote) and the result (Carried, Carried Unanimously, Lost). 83,824 lines as of November 11, 2026, refreshed by the City about every two weeks.
  • The City’s own note on this data set reads: “Please consult corresponding meeting minutes for official vote records.” The minutes are published separately by the City Clerk; this site does not read them.
  • The data set is published under the Open Government Licence – Vancouver, version 1.0, read in full on November 15, 2026. It grants “a worldwide, royalty-free, perpetual, non-exclusive licence to use the Information, including for commercial purposes”, on two conditions that concern this site: to acknowledge the source — hence the line at the foot of every page, “Contains information licensed under the Open Government Licence – Vancouver” — and never to suggest that the City endorses this site or that it has any official status. It does not.

What the data does not contain

  • The text of the decisions. The City publishes the agenda line of each item (about fifty characters), not the motion, not the report, not the minutes. That is why there are no plain-language summaries in this section, unlike the Québec and Montréal sections: there is nothing to summarize honestly, and we write nothing in its place. The City’s archival data set of council minutes (“council-meeting-minutes-archival”) has been empty since 2014.
  • Meetings without a recorded vote. A meeting appears here only if at least one item was put to an electronic vote. A meeting where everything passed by consent, or a meeting in camera, does not exist in this data and therefore does not exist here.
  • Districts. Vancouver elects its Council at large — the whole city elects the Mayor and the ten Councillors. There are no wards, so there is no map, and no “your councillor” to point to.
  • Parties, portfolios, contact details. The data set gives a member’s title, an initial and a family name, exactly as shown on each card (“Councillor P Fry”). Nothing else is published in it, so nothing else is shown.

What a machine does, and what it can miss

  • Grouping. The City’s lines are grouped by meeting and by vote number into one card per motion. The names under “For”, “Against”, “Abstained”, “Absent” and “Declared conflict” are the City’s own values, copied as they are. No name is extracted from a text.
  • The subject tags (zoning, grants, transportation…) do not exist in the City’s data: they are a classification added here, by keywords, on the agenda line alone. With fifty characters to go on, it is coarser than in Montréal, where the whole decision is read. A wrong tag is a mistake of ours, not of the City.
  • The “path” of an item. When several votes share the same agenda line — a withdrawal motion, an amendment, then the motion as approved — the cards are linked as one file. The link is made on the wording, so a differently worded follow-up will not be caught.
  • Times. The City records the start time of each vote in UTC; the hour shown on a meeting is converted to Vancouver time.
  • “No Vote”. The City changed the definition of this status on September 21, 2021 (see its change log). Earlier lines may not mean the same thing as later ones.

Things to know

  • This is a preview. It covers June 1 to July 31, 2026 — the last two months in which Council sat before the municipal election of October 17, 2026. In the City’s data, September and November 2026 have no vote at all, and October has eleven, on a single item: Council was in recess. The window is a setting of our tool; the City’s data goes back to April 5, 2016.
  • Unanimous does not mean everyone was there. “Carried Unanimously” means every member who voted was in favour. Absent members are listed as such on the card.
  • The same item can be voted twice: once in a standing committee (Policy & Strategic Priorities, City Finance & Services), then again when Council makes it final. Both votes are real, and both are shown.
  • What we read before using the data. The licence, in full. The data set’s description and change notes. And the portal’s robots.txt, which asks generic robots not to crawl /api/. That file governs indexing by search engines; the API is the portal’s documented interface for programs, and the licence allows the use. We note it here all the same, and the “Data status” table below says which door was used for this preview.

What we asked the City, and what it answered

Nothing yet. The licence requires no permission, and this preview has not been published. If this section goes live, we will write to the City Clerk’s office to say what we read, how often, and how to reach us — as we did in Montréal — and this paragraph will say what came back.
